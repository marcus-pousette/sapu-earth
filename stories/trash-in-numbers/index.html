INDONESIA'S TRASH, BY THE NUMBERS

Six numbers about Indonesia's trash, all from the government's own data. Sapu, our tired field reporter, is also the Earth, and takes all of this personally. Every source is linked under its number.

0 of 420

In February 2026 the environment ministry (KLH) graded 420 regencies and cities for Adipura, Indonesia's clean city trophy. None of them won it. 35 got a certificate for being on the way to clean.

Sources: KLH, 25 Feb 2026 · Antara · RRI

142,000 tonnes a day

That is the ministry's own count of Indonesia's trash, as it told parliament in April 2026: 141,926 tonnes a day. Shared out over about 285 million people, it is about half a kilo each, every day. A mango weighs about a quarter of a kilo, so: two mangoes.

Sources: Antara · Metro TV, both on KLH's numbers at the DPR hearing of 6 April 2026

1 in 4

Of those 141,926 tonnes, 37,001 a day were managed properly: 26%. The minister said that is up from about 10% in 2024, mostly because some landfills stopped open dumping.

Sources: Antara · Metro TV

57%

In the health ministry's 2023 Indonesian Health Survey (SKI), 57.2% of households said they burn their trash. Only 27.6% said a collector takes it away to a landfill.

Sources: Databoks · GoodStats, both on SKI 2023

2013

Indonesia's 2008 waste law (UU 18/2008, Pasal 44) gave regional governments five years to close landfills that are open dumps: trash piled up in the open, with no lining and no cover. That was 2013. In January 2026 the ministry counted about two in three of Indonesia's roughly 550 landfills as still open dumping. Since 1 August 2026 the ministry has banned open dumping at every landfill.

Sources: UU 18/2008 · DPR research brief, March 2025 · Kompas, 13 Feb 2026 · Kompas, 8 Sep 2026

9,450 tonnes a day

Of the 37,001 tonnes managed each day, 9,450 were handled by the informal sector: waste pickers and collectors, often by hand. That is about a quarter of all the trash that gets managed in Indonesia.

Sources: Metro TV · Antara

HOW WE COUNTED

  • Half a kilo: 141,926 tonnes a day, divided by about 285 million people, is 0.50 kg per person.
  • Two mangoes: a mango weighs about 250 grams.
  • A quarter, twice: 37,001 of 141,926 tonnes is 26%, and 9,450 of 37,001 tonnes is also 26%.
  • Each number is what the source said on the date shown. They change as regions report. Where sources differ, we used the ministry's own.
