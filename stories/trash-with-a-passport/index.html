TRASH WITH A PASSPORT

Six numbers about the plastic waste other countries ship to Indonesia, all from official data in Indonesia and the EU. Sapu, our tired field reporter, is also the Earth, so for Sapu nothing is ever thrown away: it only moves. Every source is linked under its number.

262,900 tonnes

That is how much plastic waste Indonesia imported in 2024, by the count of Statistics Indonesia (BPS): plastic scrap shipped in from abroad, to be recycled here, worth US$105 million. It was 4% more than the 252,300 tonnes of 2023. Spread over the year, it is about 720 tonnes a day.

Sources: GoodStats, on BPS data · RRI

3 in 4

Three in every four of those tonnes came from three countries in Europe: 107,500 from the Netherlands, 59,100 from Germany and 28,800 from Belgium. Europe's own numbers say the same from the other side: in 2023 Indonesia took 19% of all the plastic waste the EU exported, and 17% of its waste paper (Eurostat).

Sources: GoodStats, on BPS and Eurostat data · RRI

1 January 2025

From that day Indonesia banned plastic waste imports. The environment ministry (KLH) stopped issuing the import recommendations that importers need, for every kind of plastic waste, sorted or not. “Next year there will be no more plastic imports. Enough,” the minister said in October 2024, since Indonesia already has plenty of plastic of its own that is not managed well. In March 2025 the ministry's director of waste management confirmed that the ban had come into force on 1 January.

Sources: Antara, 30 Oct 2024 · The Diplomat, 4 Mar 2025

215,000 tonnes

That is how much plastic waste the EU sent to Indonesia in 2025, the year the ban began, by the European Commission's own count. Only Türkiye and Malaysia (276,000 tonnes) got more. Germany alone exported more than 810,000 tonnes of plastic waste that year, most of it to Türkiye, Malaysia and Indonesia, an investigation reported in May 2026. The EU counts what leaves Europe. We have not found Indonesia's own count for 2025 yet, so how much of it got in, and how, these numbers do not say.

Sources: European Commission, 18 Sep 2026 · Kompas, 4 May 2026

21 November 2026

From this day the EU bans all exports of plastic waste to countries outside the OECD, Indonesia among them, until 21 May 2029 at least. After that, a country can take EU plastic waste again only if it asks to, and shows the European Commission that it can handle it safely. The ban is part of the EU's Waste Shipment Regulation of 2024.

Sources: European Commission, plastic waste shipments · Regulation (EU) 2024/1157

2.8 million tonnes

Waste paper still comes in. Indonesia's paper mills import about 2.8 million tonnes of it a year, 40% of the recycled paper they use, the industry minister said in November 2026. The rules allow at most 2% other trash in it. Ecoton, a river research group in East Java, has found up to 40% plastic and hazardous waste in one container of imported paper, and the head of the paper industry's association has said that some mills dump what they pick out on open land. Waste paper is also the one kind of EU waste that the European Commission's draft list of September 2026 would let Indonesia keep taking after May 2027.

Sources: Kontan, 4 Nov 2026 · CNN Indonesia, 26 Aug 2024 · European Commission, draft list · CIRS, 21 Sep 2026

HOW WE COUNTED

  • About 720 tonnes a day: 262,900 tonnes divided by the 366 days of 2024.
  • Three in four: 107,500 + 59,100 + 28,800 = 195,400 of 262,900 tonnes, which is 74%.
  • Two sides, two counts: the EU counts the plastic waste it sends, Indonesia counts what arrives, and the two do not always match. The 215,000 tonnes is the EU's count.
  • Each number is what its source said on the date shown. Where sources differ, we used the official ones.
